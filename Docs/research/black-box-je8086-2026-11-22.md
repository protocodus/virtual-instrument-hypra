# Black-box measurements of JE-8086, 2026-11-22

JE-8086 2.2.27 (The Usual Suspects) emulates the JP-8000's DSPs and runs
Roland's system software, here version 1.05 from the owner's update files.
The owner installed it and supplied those files. It was used as an
instrument only: patches in, notes in, audio out. Its source code, its
technical write-up and the talk about it were not read, and no code or data
from it or from the system software is in this repository. What follows are
measurements of sound.

## Method

The plug-in was hosted from a script with its editor open and fed in
real time at 88.2 kHz. Each capture sets part A to a neutral patch plus the
values under test, plays one note and records it. 180 captures: 17 values of
CTRL 1 for saw, triangle, triangle mod and pulse; -12 and -24 dB low-pass
sweeps of cutoff and resonance on a plain saw at C1; high- and band-pass
spot checks; supersaw notes. Harmonic amplitudes were read at exact multiples
of the measured pitch.

Check against hardware: its SHAPE 0 saw has harmonics 2.23, 1.31, 1.13 times
a plain saw's, where two recordings of real units gave 2.25, 1.31, 1.14.

## Findings

| Mechanism | Measured |
| --- | --- |
| Pulse width | duty 0.5 + v / 256; mean removed; constant peak to peak |
| Saw SHAPE | saw + K x (saw harmonics / n^2); K = 1.267 at 0, 0 at 32, -3.52 at 127, near linear on each side |
| Triangle mod | triangle x g, wrapped at the rails; g = 2.1175 - v / 128, held between 1.375 and 2.015 |
| Levels against the plain saw | pulse 0.82, triangle 1.60, triangle mod 0.86 |
| Filter structure | all-pole two-pole section of the Chamberlin form at 88.2 kHz, fit to 0.02 dB; band and high outputs for BPF and HPF |
| Cutoff | coefficient 0.039016 (548 Hz) at 64, a semitone per step (measured x 10 per 40 steps), stopping at 1 from about 120 |
| Resonance | damping q = 0.993, 0.639, 0.287, 0.0748, 0.0209, about 0.004 at 0, 32, 64, 96, 112, 127 |
| -24 dB | two identical sections, output scaled by q |
| -12 dB level | unity to resonance 64, then -3.0, -5.1, -5.9 dB |
| Full resonance | a long ring, not a sustained oscillation, with silent input |

## Round 2, 2026-11-23: timing and modulation

Same host and harness. 361 takes in three sweeps (`cap3`, `cap4`, `cap5`
in the session scratchpad; nothing captured is in the repository).
Envelope times were read from a triangle at C4 (peak per cycle), pitch
from rising zero crossings of a triangle, and cutoff from the peak of
white noise (NOISE, ctrl 127/0) through the 12 dB filter at resonance 112.
That cutoff meter reproduces the round-1 cutoff law within 1 % from CUTOFF
24 up (below that the peak sits under 55 Hz), which is an independent check
of round 1. The emulator's latency is 95.7 ms. One harness fault: the first
note after a patch with SUSTAIN 0 is sometimes dropped, so decay takes use
a throwaway first note.

### Findings

- **One rate table for every envelope stage.** DECAY and RELEASE give the
  same exponential (fits within 0.03 dB from 48 up), the same time for the
  same value within 2.5 %. The time to fall 60 dB was fitted every fourth
  value from 12 to 112 and at every value at both ends. Predicting the
  in-between values from every eighth one misses by 1 % median, so the
  table is interpolated geometrically. The top climbs in groups of four,
  with a jump from 21.5 s at 116 to 55 s at 117, and 101 s at 127.
- **The attack is the same exponential**, heading for 1.2525 and stopping
  at 1: the fitted curve stays within 0.2 % of full level from 40 to 127,
  and its time to the top is 0.232 times the release's 60 dB time at every
  value from 32 to 127, which is ln(1.2525 / 0.2525) / ln(1000). So the
  hardware runs one time constant per value for all three stages.
- **The filter and pitch envelopes use the same table** (filter: attack,
  decay and release at 16, 48, 80, 112; pitch decay at 0 to 127).
- **Below 10 the release is two-stage**: 20 to 30 dB in a few ms, then a
  tail of about -6 dB per 20 ms. Trancera keeps only the fast part. 0 and 1 cut
  within one cycle.
- **LFO 1 RATE**, every fourth value: 0.050 Hz at 0, 3.2 Hz at 64, 22.7 Hz
  at 124 and a jump to 50 Hz at 127.
- **Pitch envelope depth** peaks at 14.6 semitones at +32 and 48.3 (four
  octaves) at +63, symmetric for negative values. **LFO 1 pitch depth**
  swings half of that each way (measured 2-4 % short, within the
  tracker's rounding of the triangle's corners).
- **Filter envelope depth** shifts the cutoff by 36.1 steps at +32 and 120
  (the whole range) at +63, symmetric. **LFO 1 filter depth** is half of it
  (the meter is noisy here; within 2 steps).
- **Key follow** pivots at C4. +32 is 100 % and +63 200 %, but -64 is only
  -100 %: the negative side is value/64. OM-8000 p.61 prints -64 as -200 %.
- **Portamento** is a straight line in pitch that takes the same time for
  5, 12 and 24 semitones, up or down: 0.316 s at 64, 9 s at 127.

### Trancera 0.3.0 against the captures

`deliverables/ab-je8086-round2-2026-11-23/measured-gaps.txt` lists 37
checks on new renders against the takes. Median gap 1.0 %. Release, decay,
LFO rate, pitch and filter depths and key follow are within 3.3 %. The
remaining gaps:

- Attack below 40 (t50 off by 14 % at 40) and the first 10-15 ms of slower
  attacks: the emulator starts its attack about 13 ms late from 64 up, and
  its release about 25 ms late from 32 to 96. Not modelled.
- Portamento lands 4-8 ms later than the emulator, whose glide starts
  about 4 ms before the note in these takes.
- Pitch envelope decay at 32: 10 % slow on the half-way time.

## Round 3, 2026-11-23: modulation, noise and cross mod

145 takes (`cap6`, scratchpad only). Fast envelope segments were read on a
triangle at C6 (one cycle is 1 ms). The pitch and cutoff meters are the
round-2 ones; X-MOD was fitted by matching band spectra to a synthetic
triangle-on-triangle FM.

### Findings

- **Envelope segments start late.** Decay and release hold for 7.5 ms from
  10 to 29 and for 25 ms from 30 up (the round-2 fits of every fourth value
  show the step between 28 and 32; above about 96 the delay is too small
  to measure). The attack holds 14 ms from 30 up and not at all below.
  Below 30 the attack is also faster than the table (geometric from 8 to
  28).
- **FADE** raises LFO 1's depth along a straight line in decibels, from
  37 dB down to full, in the envelope table's 60 dB time divided by 4.45.
- **RANDOM** takes a new value once a cycle. Saw falls then jumps, as the
  manual says. LFO 1 runs free across notes.
- **LFO 2** is a triangle: 0.78 Hz at 0, an octave every 25.6 steps, 50 Hz
  at 127. Its depths use LFO 1's curves, scaled linearly by the lever.
- **Amp LFO depth** (LFO 1 and 2) swings the gain 29 % each way at 32 and
  95 % at 63, on the pitch-depth curve.
- **Pan:** the near side gains 3.2 dB at ±32 and stays there; the far side
  falls to half at ±32 and to silence at the end. AUTO swings all the way at
  63.
- **OSC BALANCE** is a constant-power crossfade, -3 dB each in the centre.
  OM-8000 p.58 suggests both at full.
- **X-MOD** is linear, through-zero FM in Hz: OSC 2's ±1 waveform moves
  OSC 1 by 69 Hz a step (8.8 kHz at 127), the same at C3, C4 and C5 and with
  OSC 2 a fifth or an octave up.
- **Noise** is white noise through the main filter's -24 dB shape (two
  identical sections, the same resonance table). The coefficient is 2π times
  the played note's frequency over the engine rate at CUTOFF 64, a semitone
  a step: within 2 % from 48 to 120 and on C3 to C6. The level stays within
  1 dB as RESONANCE goes up. Below about 32 it is silent.
- **Velocity** does nothing with the velocity matrix at its defaults (all
  zero); level and filter are unchanged from velocity 1 to 127.

### Trancera 0.4.0 against the captures

`deliverables/ab-je8086-round3-2026-11-23/measured-gaps.txt`: 40 checks,
median gap 1.2 %. Envelope starts, fade, LFO 2, amp LFO, pan, balance and
the noise coefficient are within 6.4 %. Remaining:

- Attack 16 runs 12-22 % slow, at the meter's 1 ms resolution.
- The noise filter's fitted damping at RESONANCE 0 is 10 % above the main
  filter's table; left on the table.
- X-MOD band spectra differ by 5-6 dB rms (2.9 dB with X-MOD off, mostly the
  emulator's noise floor); the strong components are within 1.5 dB.

## Round 4, 2026-11-23: supersaw, ring and feedback oscillator

About 260 takes (`cap7` to `cap10`, scratchpad only). One harness fault
mattered: an OSC 1 control set in the same patch change as a new waveform
can land on the old waveform. The per-value Detune takes therefore ran at
MIX 0 (checked: centre 24-28 dB over the sides), which does not affect the
spread; the feedback takes were rerun with every control re-sent.

### Findings

- **Detune**, every value from 34: Szabo's points hold within 0.2 % at the
  points, but the curve between them is not straight. It steps in pairs
  from 34 to 61 (2 % swings) and climbs steeply from 120: 0.424 at 121,
  0.524 at 124, 0.699 at 126, where straight lines gave up to 47 % more.
- **Mix** at every Szabo point: centre against sides within 0.3 dB. The
  supersaw was 1.4 dB loud against the saw; corrected.
- **Ring modulation**: the same sidebands (f2 ± f1 and their odd
  products), 3.6 dB quieter than Trancera had it.
- **Feedback oscillator**: the howl sits exactly on a harmonic of the note
  (a period-long loop, not 2.5 samples short as a wet factory recording
  suggested). HARMONICS picks the harmonic in a jumpy pattern (2, 16, 7,
  32, 41, 8 ... at 0, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32 ...), the same on every key. The
  howl starts at AMOUNT 16 and is near full strength by 48.
- **Triangle SHAPE** above 0 is skewed (its peaks move closer together,
  19/48 of a cycle at 32) as well as sharpened, and folds above 40. Neither
  a sine of a cubic nor a sine of a powered triangle fits (relative error
  0.3-0.7). Left as it was.

### Trancera 0.5.0 against the captures

`deliverables/ab-je8086-round4-2026-11-23`: supersaw pairs level-match
within 0.1 dB, ring within 1.4 dB, feedback within 2 dB, with the howl on
the same harmonic. The emulator's howl carries the odd harmonics of a
square (4, 12, 20 x f0); Trancera's band-passed howl is purer above it.

## Round 5, 2026-11-23: tone control, delay and chorus

58 takes (`cap11`, `cap12`, scratchpad only), stereo, white noise for the
tone control and a 10 ms click for the delay.

- **TONE CONTROL** is two first-order shelves. TREBLE turns above about
  940 Hz: +9.8 dB at 32, +14.6 dB at 63 (still rising at 16 kHz). BASS
  turns below about 435 Hz: +6.5 dB at 32 and a much steeper +28.7 dB shelf
  at 63, which is still rising at 31 Hz. Cuts mirror boosts. Trancera had
  ±12 dB shelves at 250 Hz and 4 kHz.
- **DELAY TIME** (MONO LONG): 25 ms at 16, 71 ms at 32, 0.2 s at 64, 0.6 s
  at 112 and 1.25 s at 127. The panning types run at half that (0.1 s at
  64, 0.625 s at 127), the short types at about an eighth (0.153 s at 127).
  PANNING SHORT answers on the right first.
- **FEEDBACK** is square-law: each repeat loses 2.2 dB at 127, 7 dB at 96,
  14 dB at 64. **Echo level** at LEVEL 127: -2.3 dB (MONO LONG), -2.9 dB
  (panning), -3.5 dB (short types) against the dry click.
- **Chorus**: FREEZE PHASE 1 is a comb at 11.6 ms (left) and 12.6 ms
  (right) with light feedback; FREEZE PHASE 2 a faint comb near 0.9 ms. The
  modulated types could not be pinned with these takes: rates read from
  harmonic beating disagree between runs, and their combs smear.

Harness note: in the feedback and level sweeps the TIME stayed at an older
value (25 ms instead of 32 ms), so only the type and time sweeps were
used for times.

## Factory patches, 2026-11-23

Ten factory programs (part A, forced to SINGLE, arpeggiator off) played the
same phrase on JE-8086 and on Trancera 0.6.1 with the same settings, read back
from the plugin after each program change (`Tools/blackbox/presets_ab.py`,
`presets_map.py`, `presets_cmp.py`). Results are in
`deliverables/ab-factory-patches-2026-11-23/KEY.txt`.

- **Amp SUSTAIN is square-law** (64 plays 11.9 dB down; every 16th value
  within 0.1 dB). Trancera had it linear. The filter SUSTAIN is linear, as
  Trancera had it. AMP LEVEL is square-law, as Trancera had it.
- Close (within about 2 dB in level and tone): Euro SAW, Soaring Mini above
  300 Hz, JP-303 below 3 kHz, Proflike Clavit, Spit'n Slide Bs.
- **The velocity and control matrices** (not in Trancera) explain Silk 5ths and
  Old Rhodes: with them switched off on JE-8086 both come within 2.2 dB.
- Unexplained, to chase next: Dreams Are Made (Trancera far darker; cutoff 0,
  resonance 55, filter env decay 0 / sustain 61, key follow -40, OSC SHIFT
  -2, TWISTING), Moody Organ (Trancera 7.4 dB loud; triangle with OSC 2 synced,
  X-MOD destination), Sine Lead (6 dB quiet), Soaring Mini below 300 Hz
  (8 dB thin), JP-303 above 3 kHz (6.6 dB bright).

### Following up the differences (0.6.2)

Each problem patch was played as one held note with one setting changed at a
time on both (`Tools/blackbox/variants.py`), which pinned the causes:

- **Key follow tracks the pitch after OSC SHIFT.** Dreams Are Made (OSC
  SHIFT -2, key follow -40) is 15 steps brighter on JE-8086 than Trancera made
  it; with OSC SHIFT 0 or key follow 0 the two agreed. Fixed; this also
  fixed Sine Lead (OSC SHIFT +1), now within 1-2 dB.
- **LFO 1 to X-MOD is on the pitch-depth curve.** Moody Organ (destination
  X-MOD, depth -3) showed no sidebands on JE-8086; Trancera had added up to
  4.7 % of X-MOD (412 Hz of FM), a sub-octave growl. Fixed; Moody Organ
  went from +7.4 to +2.4 dB.
- **Effect levels are square-law.** DELAY LEVEL echoes at 32, 64, 96, 127
  sit -28.8, -17.0, -9.9, -5.0 dB under the dry note. MULTI-FX LEVEL adds
  little below 64 on every type, and each type adds a different amount at
  127 (from -0.2 dB for SUPER CHORUS FST to +4.1 dB for MID on a held
  triangle). Trancera now uses square-law levels and a per-type wet gain fitted
  to that (MID kept at 1).
- Single held notes now agree within about 3 dB in every band for JP-303,
  Sine Lead, Moody Organ, Dreams Are Made (dry) and Soaring Mini above its
  fundamental.

Still open after 0.6.2:
- The velocity and control matrices (Silk 5ths, Old Rhodes, part of Dreams
  Are Made). Trancera has neither.
- JP-303's lead line is up to 10 dB brighter above 3 kHz on some notes in
  Trancera, though a held note matches exactly. The patch is mono, legato,
  with portamento; suspect how a note retriggers the filter envelope after
  a short gap.
- Dreams Are Made's TWISTING plus delay adds highs (4-8 kHz) on JE-8086 that
  Trancera's chorus does not.
- Soaring Mini has 6-7 dB more content below its fundamental on JE-8086
  (around -40 dB, likely aliasing or the supersaw's high-pass).

## Factory patches on 0.8.0, 2026-11-23

The same ten programs and phrase against the same JE-8086 captures, rendered
by 0.7.1 (`a00a1ca`) and by 0.8.0 (`6733193`'s exact algorithms, renamed),
with `Tools/blackbox/presets_cmp.py` (`RENDER` and `LABEL` pick the build).
Raw level is ours minus the emulator's; tone is per region after level
matching; the full tables are `deliverables/ab-factory-patches-0.8.0-2026-11-23/*/KEY.txt`.

With the emulator's velocity and control matrices off (Trancera has neither),
every patch now sits within 1.0 dB in level (0.7.1: up to 3.0 dB) and within
2.7 dB in every region except Soaring Mini below 300 Hz (-6.6 dB, -7.9 on
0.7.1). Dreams Are Made went from -9.6 / -42.8 dB (mid / highs) to +2.0 /
-0.1; JP-303's highs from +6.6 to -0.3; Moody Organ's mids from -4.3 to +1.5;
Silk 5ths, Old Rhodes and Sine Lead sit within 1.2 dB everywhere.
The 10 ms envelopes match a little better on seven of ten.

With the matrices on, the gaps that remain are theirs: Silk 5ths above 3 kHz
(-19.0 dB), Proflike Clavit above 3 kHz (-8.1), Dreams Are Made (-7.9 mid,
-10.3 high) and Old Rhodes (-5.1 mid, -3.2 level).

Still open on 0.8.0: Soaring Mini's content below its fundamental, Spit'n
Slide Bs below 300 Hz (-2.7 dB), Euro SAW below 300 Hz (-2.0 to -3.1 dB),
and the velocity and control matrices.
